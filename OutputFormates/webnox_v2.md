# Webnox Team v2 — Evidence-Led Website Content Output Format

## 1. Purpose and authority

This file defines **how final website copy is structured and presented** for the Webnox Team v2 format. It is a presentation and page-architecture specification, not a replacement for the main `SKILL.md`, keyword research, Google Ads Keyword Planner evidence, competitor research, audit findings, content brief, brand voice, or fact-checking rules.

The main `SKILL.md` remains authoritative for research, factuality, keyword selection, claims, technical SEO, internal linking, self-critique, and publication readiness. This file controls the final page-copy shape when the user selects **Webnox Team v2**.

### Non-negotiable rules

1. **Research determines the page; the template does not.** Choose a page pattern that matches the search intent, customer, service/product, target market, and conversion objective.
2. **Use the reference pack to learn structure, not to borrow facts.** Never reuse its company claims, numbers, product specifications, awards, testimonials, guarantees, offers, locations, customer names, URLs, or performance statistics for a new client/page unless separately verified for that project.
3. **No invented evidence.** If a proof point is missing, request it, omit it, or mark it in the internal draft as `[SOURCE REQUIRED: describe missing evidence]`. Do not leave working placeholders in copy labeled publish-ready.
4. **No competitor copying.** Competitor pages may inform information architecture, buyer questions, content coverage and UX ideas. Do not copy their wording, distinctive sequence wholesale, claims, testimonials, visuals, or proprietary specifications.
5. **Do not force every section onto every page.** Combine patterns only when research justifies it. Remove sections that do not help the reader decide, understand, trust, or act.
6. **Keep one center of gravity.** One page should have one primary commercial purpose. Supporting industries, locations, use cases or technologies must not overpower that purpose.
7. **Separate page copy from research artifacts.** Keyword matrices, competitor comparisons, audit reports, evidence ledgers and internal critique remain separate deliverables unless the user asks to include them.
8. **Make the output implementation-ready.** Label H1/H2/H3/H4, section intent, CTAs and asset notes clearly so SEO, content, design and development teams can hand off the page without guessing.

- An **industrial equipment/manufacturer page** for a block-bottom bag-making machine: keyword list, URL/title/meta, machine capability description, throughput and material specifications, features, service/support, testimonials, price/quote intent and configuration FAQs.

The operational thinking below is inferred from the written structures and review notes. It is not a claim about the private thought process of any individual SEO professional.

## 3. The underlying page-planning logic

Before drafting, resolve these decisions from the approved content brief. Do not print this planning checklist in the final page copy unless requested.

### 3.1 Identify the page's primary job

State the intended result in one line, for example:

- Generate a qualified call or booking.
- Get an industrial buyer to request a quote or factory visit.
- Help a B2B buyer compare service options and request a consultation.
- Explain a core company offer and route visitors to the correct service.
- Help a location-specific customer confirm coverage and take the next step.
- Help a buyer determine whether a product/configuration fits their production requirements.

### 3.2 Bind the page to search intent

Use observed SERPs, keyword data, audience research and the supplied brief to classify the dominant intent. One page may have secondary intents, but the dominant one leads the content hierarchy.

| Intent pattern | What the page must help the reader do | Sections that usually earn priority |
|---|---|---|
| Local service + location | Confirm provider coverage, trust, service availability and contact route | Location-aware hero, services, local proof, service process/coverage, practical FAQs, call/booking CTA |
| Service / solution | Understand what is offered, whom it is for and how to engage | Outcome-led hero, service scope, use cases/capabilities, proof, process, differentiators, FAQs, consultation CTA |
| Industry + service | Understand how the offer fits a specific industry's workflow | Industry context, specific pain points, industry use cases, integration/process, relevant proof, risk/compliance caveats where verified |
| Industrial product / equipment | Confirm capability, suitability, specifications, configuration and after-sales support | Product identity, operating outcome, specs, materials/capacity, feature-to-benefit mapping, applications, service/support, quote CTA |
| “Price”, “cost”, “quote” | Understand cost drivers and how to get an accurate price | Pricing model or verified range if available, variables affecting price, what is included, configuration/assessment process, quote CTA |
| Comparison / alternatives | Make a fair and informed selection | Comparison criteria, use-case fit, evidence-sourced table, trade-offs, migration/implementation questions, decision CTA |
| Company/homepage | Understand the company's primary positioning and navigate to the correct capability | One primary positioning, services overview, selected proof, representative projects, differentiators, audience fit, navigation CTAs |
| Informational / educational | Get a clear answer and understand what to do next | Direct answer, concept breakdown, process/options, examples with sources, limitations, related resources, soft CTA appropriate to stage |

Do not build a location page, industry page, comparison page or price page merely because a keyword exists. It must represent a real user need and a useful, distinct page purpose.

### 3.3 Translate buyer intent into a page promise

The H1, hero description and opening paragraph should work together:

- **H1:** identify the main service/product/topic and its primary audience or outcome.
- **Hero subheading:** explain what the company/product actually helps the user accomplish.
- **Intro:** connect the need, the offer and the practical result without repeating the H1.
- **CTA:** ask for the next action that fits the buyer's stage and the actual sales process.

Avoid generic promises such as “innovative solutions for all your needs” when the page can state the actual work, user, system, product or result.

### 3.4 Build the page around evidence and decisions

For every major section, be able to answer at least one question:

- What does the buyer need to know here?
- What decision or objection does this section address?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- How does this section differ from the one above it?
- What should the reader do or understand next?

If a section answers none of these, remove it or redesign it.

## 4. Evidence-derived layout principles

### 4.1 Hero: specific promise first

The samples repeatedly begin with a clear page identity and a compact promise. Local and B2B examples add a location or audience cue; the industrial example leads with the machine category and measurable operating capability where substantiated; the hosting example communicates the service choices and operating benefit.

Use the following order where relevant:

1. Optional eyebrow/pre-heading: location, audience, product class or specialist context.
2. One H1, carrying the main topic naturally.
3. One concise subheading describing the deliverable or customer outcome.
4. A short introduction only if the subheading is insufficient.
5. A small proof/benefit strip when the items are verified and meaningful.
6. Primary CTA plus optional secondary CTA.
7. Optional supporting asset note: product image, service image, customer logo strip, interface screenshot, or other approved visual.

Keep the first screen focused. Do not add several competing headline ideas or an oversized list of every service the company offers.

### 4.2 Use proof to reduce uncertainty

The source examples use different proof formats: years of experience, clients/countries served, awards, product specifications, brand logos, service guarantees, testimonials, project summaries and process capabilities. These are different kinds of evidence and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Prioritize the proof most relevant to the buyer's decision:

- Local service: actual service coverage, availability, qualifications, warranty terms, authentic reviews and clear pricing policy.
- Industrial equipment: verified throughput, dimensions, tolerances, material compatibility, supported configurations, installation and maintenance support.
- B2B/technology services: real delivered projects, integration scope, evaluation method, implementation steps, security controls and verifiable outcomes.
- Company/homepage: verified experience, representative clients/projects, relevant capabilities and credible organizational details.

Do not add an arbitrary number strip because a template has one. Avoid “best”, “leading”, “fastest”, “guaranteed”, “zero downtime”, “100%”, “industry-first” and similar claims unless the wording is defensible and the evidence is approved.

### 4.3 Feature → function → buyer value

A strong section does more than list features. Explain what the feature does, in which situation it matters, and what it helps the buyer achieve. Use this sequence when suitable:

- **Capability/feature:** what the service, machine or system includes.
- **Operational meaning:** what it does in the workflow or environment.
- **Buyer relevance:** why that matters for the target user.
- **Constraint or fit:** where it applies, what options are available, or what needs confirmation.

For technical specifications, present units and scope clearly. Never infer or round up performance specifications. If specs vary by configuration, say so and request confirmation.

### 4.4 Organize offerings into useful groups

The reference pages use service grids, product cards and capability groups. Each card should represent a real and distinct option—not a synonym created for keyword variation.

Recommended card contract:

- `H3: [Distinct service, product type or capability]`
- 1–3 sentences stating fit/use case, important capability and practical value.
- Optional short specifications or “Best for” line, where relevant.
- Optional CTA that names the next step for that specific item.

Do not duplicate the same offering under different headings unless the user journey genuinely needs different entry points.

### 4.5 Use process sections to make delivery tangible

The robotics and hosting examples explain work through named stages, from site visit/configuration through implementation and ongoing support, or from assessment through migration and verification. This helps the buyer understand what happens after they submit a form.

For each step, include:

- Step number and action-led name.
- What the team/client does.
- What is produced, checked, installed or decided.
- Any dependency or approval that materially affects the step.

Use the real process. Do not claim that the team provides site visits, installation, migration, training, support, or a fixed timeline unless confirmed.

### 4.6 Cover objections before asking for conversion

Useful objection-handling sections may address:

- Fit and eligibility: which customer, appliance, machine, workload or platform is supported?
- Scope: what is included and excluded?
- Technical fit: materials, systems, integrations, configuration, deployment, or compatibility.
- Risk: warranty, safety, security, downtime, review, maintenance or support arrangements.
- Cost: variables that determine price, quote process, and what is known/not known.
- Implementation: what the process requires from the customer and what happens next.

Choose the objections relevant to the actual page and buyer. Avoid inserting generic “benefits” sections to fill space.

### 4.7 FAQs answer real questions

The source examples include practical questions about same-day service, pricing, warranty, repair/replace decisions, production consistency, materials, configuration, hosting choice, hosting differences, migration and cost.

Use questions found in relevant SERPs/PAA, customer calls, sales conversations, support tickets or validated keyword data. Each answer should:

- answer the question immediately;
- include the qualification or condition that changes the answer;
- avoid repeating an entire earlier section;
- avoid promises that sales/operations have not approved;
- direct the user to a helpful next step only where relevant.

There is no mandatory FAQ count. Omit an FAQ section if the page has no useful unanswered questions. Do not create FAQs solely to insert keywords, and do not promise rich-result eligibility.

### 4.8 CTA placement should follow the decision journey

The examples use a primary hero CTA and then relevant CTA opportunities after service cards, product details, proof, or the final section. Some local/B2B designs also specify call, WhatsApp, floating contact or site-visit routes.

Use these rules:

- One clear primary conversion action per page.
- A secondary CTA only when it serves a different but legitimate path (for example, call versus enquiry form, or book a visit versus request a quote).
- Card-level CTA only where the reader can take a distinct action for that item.
- Repeat the main CTA after substantial decision-making sections and at the close where useful.
- Specify floating/sticky CTA placement only as a layout note when it is useful and supported by the site design; it is not mandatory copy.
- Never promise a free consultation, quote, audit, factory visit, demo, discount or response time unless approved.

### 4.9 Visual/layout notes are part of the handoff, not proof

The reference pack contains visual layout notes—such as logo rows, forms, video versus form, award sections, product cards, testimonials and case-study placement. Use them to specify a possible content component, not to assert that the component itself improves rankings or conversion.

Use a clearly separated note such as:

`[Visual/UX note: use 3–4 product cards with specifications and a CTA per card, if this matches the approved design system.]`

Asset notes must not masquerade as page copy. Reference competitor URLs belong in the internal research or design brief, not on the published page unless a legitimate citation/comparison requires them.

## 5. Keyword, entity and heading implementation

### 5.1 Map keywords to meaning, not repetition

The primary keyword should be reflected naturally in the SEO title, H1, URL slug where appropriate, and key page copy. Supporting keywords should appear where their underlying concepts genuinely belong—service cards, applications, process, specifications, use cases, industries, FAQs or related resources.

Use keyword data and the observed SERP to decide coverage. Do not force all related keywords into the first paragraph or into every heading.

### 5.2 Heading hierarchy

- **H1:** one primary page heading; clearly identifies the page's core subject.
- **H2:** major question, decision or topic group.
- **H3:** a distinct service, product type, process step, benefit, industry application or FAQ question.
- **H4:** only for useful substructure inside an H3; avoid heading levels used merely for styling.

Headings should describe the content beneath them. A heading may include a target phrase, but natural clarity outranks awkward exact-match insertion.

### 5.3 Page title and meta description

Output:

- `URL Slug:` recommended path, subject to current URL/canonical review.
- `SEO Title:` primary subject plus meaningful differentiator or brand, within an appropriate length for the market/SERP.
- `Meta Description:` specific offer, audience or outcome and a useful next step. Treat it as a SERP snippet candidate, not a guaranteed Google display.

Never change an existing live URL just because a new slug looks cleaner. Existing URL changes need redirect/canonical and migration review through the main skill's technical workflow.

## 6. Required output conventions

Use explicit labels and a clean, paste-ready sequence. Adapt section numbers to the chosen pattern.

- `URL Slug: /.../`
- `SEO Title: ...`
- `Meta Description: ...`
- `PAGE TYPE: ...` may be included as an internal handoff label when useful; omit if the user asks for publish-ready copy only.
- `SECTION 01 — HERO` (section labels are for content/design handoff, not HTML headings)
- `Eyebrow / Pre-heading:` optional
- `H1: ...`
- `Hero subheading:`
- `Intro:`
- `Proof strip:` optional, using only approved facts
- `CTA — Primary:`
- `CTA — Secondary:` optional
- `H2: ...`, `H3: ...`, `H4: ...` for the actual page heading hierarchy
- `[Visual note: ...]` and `[Asset required: ...]` for design handoff, only when useful
- `Final CTA:` at the end where the page needs a conversion close

Use `---` between major content blocks. Use bullets for concise feature lists, numbered lists for process steps, and a Markdown table only when readers benefit from side-by-side comparison or structured specifications.

**Draft-state placeholders:** `[SOURCE REQUIRED: ...]`, `[CONFIRM: ...]`, `[APPROVED CLIENT LOGO]`, `[CASE STUDY REQUIRED]`.

Before labeling the content “publish-ready,” resolve, remove or explicitly omit every unresolved placeholder. If the user requests a planning draft, preserve open questions in a separate `EDITORIAL NOTES — NOT FOR PUBLICATION` block after the page copy.

## 7. Page-pattern selector

Select one primary pattern. Add optional blocks only where evidence supports the need. A local service page may also need an FAQ block; a machine page may include industry applications; a service page may include verified case studies. These are combinations of relevant blocks—not reasons to merge every template into a single page.

### Local-page safeguards

- A location name alone does not make a page locally useful. Include distinct, verifiable coverage, project, team, delivery or audience context.
- Do not generate near-duplicate pages by swapping city names into the same paragraphs.
- Do not claim “best in [location]”, local market leadership, response times, physical presence, on-site service, local testimonials or local references without evidence.
- Match currency, spelling, terminology, time zones and language to the target market.

### Industrial-specific writing rules

- Give units with every number and explain whether it is a maximum, typical range, configuration-dependent value or guaranteed rated specification.
- Distinguish the base machine from optional components, installation services, consumables and custom configurations.
- Connect capabilities to production tasks; do not merely stack technical terms.
- A price-intent keyword should lead to pricing factors, a verified range or a useful quote process—not a vague sales pitch.
- Never claim a machine is “zero maintenance”, “defect-free”, “the fastest” or universally compatible without strong substantiation.

## 16. How to use competitor and reference-page observations

Competitor research should lead to structured observations that influence page planning. Keep this evidence log outside the final website copy unless the user expressly requests it.

| Observation field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Source | Competitor URL and date accessed |
| Page type / intent | Local service, product, comparison, service category, homepage, etc. |
| Visible structure | Hero, proof strip, cards, specifications, process, case studies, FAQ, CTA and page-footer modules |
| Buyer question addressed | What doubt or decision does the section resolve? |
| Evidence / usefulness | Why it may be useful; separate observed structure from proven performance |
| Gap/opportunity | What is absent, too vague, poorly explained or not covered by our current site? |
| Adaptation plan | How to answer the same buyer need with original, client-specific content |

## 17. Evidence and claims control

Use the main skill's proof rules. For each factual claim in the draft, retain an internal record in the brief or evidence ledger:

| Claim / content element | Required evidence | State | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metric, performance percentage or client count | Client-approved source, analytics/report or other reliable dated evidence | Verified / User-provided / Unverified / Conflicting / Expired | Keep, qualify, re-check or remove |
| Certification, warranty, safety or compliance statement | Current certificate, terms, documented process or approval | Verified / Unverified | Include only within actual scope |
| Testimonial or logo | Original review/client approval and usage permission | Approved / Permission needed / Unavailable | Quote faithfully, obtain permission, or omit |
| Product specification | Manufacturer data sheet or responsible technical approval | Verified / Configuration-dependent / Unknown | State with units and conditions or omit |
| Local service, office or coverage claim | Current operational confirmation | Verified / Unknown | State precise coverage or omit |
| Competitor capability, price or comparison point | Current source and observation date | Verified / Incomplete / Unknown | Attribute internally, qualify, or omit |

Do not resolve conflicting numbers by picking the largest or most attractive value. Ask the owner, select one current authoritative source, or omit the number. If source evidence disagrees, treat that as an open issue rather than copyediting it away.

## 18. Content-quality rules learned from reviewing the reference set

The examples supply useful structure, but some drafts/notes also demonstrate risks. The v2 format should retain the strong structural patterns and avoid carrying forward these weaknesses.

### Keep

- Explicit URL slug, SEO title and meta description before page copy.
- Clearly labeled H1/H2/H3 hierarchy and page-section sequence.
- Hero subheading that states the actual service/product, customer and outcome.
- CTAs that match real customer actions (book, call, visit, request quote, consult or enquire).
- Concise benefit/service cards with one distinct purpose each.
- Product specifications, operating details and configuration guidance where the purchase decision depends on them.
- Trust-building content where relevant: verifiable proof, testimonials, awards, client logos, warranty/service terms, support process and case studies.
- Clear delivery steps, onboarding, installation, migration or support when these are part of the offer.
- FAQs tied to real questions about availability, price, selection, compatibility, implementation, support and risk.
- Layout notes that help the web team understand a section's likely component or required asset.

### Improve or remove

- **Unsupported superlatives:** replace “best”, “fastest”, “top” or “most trusted” with a precise supported distinction.
- **Unverified metrics:** never reproduce a percentage, customer count, output rate, warranty period, lead time or award without checking the current source.
- **Conflicting metrics:** examples in a reference set may show differing company statistics. Do not silently merge or choose between them.
- **Keyword-stuffed headings:** do not repeat “[service] in [city]” in every section. Use natural wording and distinct subtopics.
- **Duplicate offerings:** the source material includes repeated/overlapping service labels; deduplicate before writing.
- **Template cross-contamination:** do not leave an unrelated product, service or industry in a page merely because it appeared in a reused layout note. Every section must fit the page's buyer and subject.
- **Generic/local misfit:** do not attach an industry or locality to a generic paragraph without showing its real relevance.
- **Unsupported guarantee language:** qualify claims that depend on schedule, configuration, parts, provider availability or customer inputs.
- **Thin “Why Choose Us” blocks:** each point must say something concrete and distinct; do not repeat “quality, reliable, innovative, experienced” in different wording.
- **Filler FAQs:** no FAQ solely to reach a target count or to place another keyword.
- **Feature-only cards:** connect each meaningful feature to what it does or the user decision it informs.
- **Unrelated SEO targets in one page:** split distinct search intents into separate pages if the site architecture and evidence justify it.
- **Competitor structure as a ranking claim:** a visible section or design choice is an observation, not evidence that it causes ranking success.

## 19. Editorial style and paragraph craft

- Write in plain, specific, buyer-oriented language. Avoid empty openers such as “In today's fast-paced world” and unqualified claims like “revolutionary solutions”.
- Lead sections with the answer or most useful point, then provide detail.
- Use paragraph lengths appropriate to the complexity: usually one main idea per paragraph; split dense technical explanations.
- Prefer active verbs and concrete nouns. Use industry terminology where it helps the intended buyer, then clarify unfamiliar concepts.
- Vary section openings. Do not start every paragraph with the company name or the exact main keyword.
- Avoid repeating the same benefit in the hero, intro, differentiators, benefits and final CTA unless each repetition serves a distinct purpose.
- Use buyer language for headings and conversion labels, not internal team jargon.
- Keep bullets parallel and short. Avoid long mixed lists combining technology names, business benefits, processes and outcomes at the same level.
- Preserve all important conditions: “up to”, “depending on configuration”, “where supported”, “subject to availability” and other qualifiers must not be deleted if they materially affect truth.
- Adapt spelling and terms to the target market (for example, UK/Canadian vs US English) consistently.
- Do not describe a service as “certified”, “authorized”, “compliant”, “guaranteed”, “secure”, “instant” or “24/7” without evidence of the specific claim.

## 20. CTA quality standard

CTAs should be concrete, short and truthful. Choose based on the offer, buyer maturity and actual operating process.

Examples of formats (not factual promises):

- `CTA — Primary: Request a Quote`
- `CTA — Primary: Book a Consultation`
- `CTA — Primary: Book a Factory Visit` — only where offered
- `CTA — Primary: Talk to a Technical Specialist`
- `CTA — Secondary: View Case Study`
- `CTA — Secondary: Discuss Your Requirements`
- `CTA — Secondary: Call [verified phone number]`
- `CTA — Secondary: WhatsApp [verified channel]`

Do not leave generic `Click Here`, create false urgency, promise a free deliverable without approval, or use a CTA that the website cannot fulfil.

## 21. Recommended default section priority

Use this as a planning sequence, not a mandatory list:

1. Metadata and URL.
2. Hero promise + CTA.
3. Closest proof or differentiator that makes the promise credible.
4. Service/product scope and major options.
5. Use cases, applications, industries or local coverage as appropriate.
6. Technical specifications, integration or selection guidance where relevant.
7. Case studies, reviews or other proof where available.
8. Why this provider/approach fits the buyer.
9. Delivery process, onboarding or support.
10. Cost/quote/implementation considerations where search intent calls for them.
11. Useful FAQs.
12. Closing CTA.

Move sections according to buyer intent. For example, a product-led page may put specification and configuration content early; an urgent local service page may prioritize availability, service area and booking; an informational article should answer the question before presenting a service pitch.

Before returning Webnox Team v2 copy, check the following internally:

- [ ] Page type and primary intent are clear.
- [ ] URL slug, SEO title, meta description and one H1 are supplied when appropriate.
- [ ] The hero answers what is offered, for whom and why the visitor should care.
- [ ] Each H2 has a distinct job and each H3 belongs under its parent.
- [ ] Primary/supporting keywords are natural and mapped to relevant sections.
- [ ] Local, industry, competitor and product details are truly relevant—not swapped-in template text.
- [ ] All metrics, specifications, certifications, warranties, awards, testimonials, company claims and case-study outcomes have approved evidence.
- [ ] Competitor observations have informed the structure without copying their prose or implying their design proves performance.
- [ ] Feature lists explain fit/value where needed; product specs include units and conditions.
- [ ] CTAs reflect actions the business actually offers and are placed at useful decision points.
- [ ] FAQs answer real buyer questions and do not duplicate the page.
