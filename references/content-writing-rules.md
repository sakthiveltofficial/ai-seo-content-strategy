# Content Writing Rules — Humanized, SEO-Aggressive

## The Core Philosophy

For technical B2B/AI service pages, prefer the voice of a practitioner explaining a difficult problem, relevant trade-offs and a defensible approach to a technical buyer. Adapt the voice to the actual audience and brand; never imply first-hand experience or production work that the author/company cannot substantiate.

Avoid generic content-calendar filler and vague AI-generated phrasing. When first-hand experience is unavailable, be transparent and support explanations with credible sources rather than pretending to have shipped the work.

---

## The Humanization Checklist

Use this editorial checklist before final review; apply each item when it fits the content, audience and brand voice:

☐ No sentence starts with "In today's world...", "As AI continues to...", "In the era of..."
☐ No consecutive sentences of the same length
☐ No more than 3 consecutive sentences starting with the same word type
☐ Every paragraph has a mix of short (under 12 words) and long (20+ words) sentences
☐ At least one paragraph uses a real example, scenario, or "here's what this actually means..."
☐ No more than 2 em-dashes per 500 words
☐ No "seamlessly", "robust", "leverage" (in business context), "synergy"
☐ Every CTA is specific to the page's offer — not generic "Contact Us"
☐ The H1 would make sense as a Google search result — a person would want to click it
☐ Opener paragraph does not start with a question

---

## Sentence Pattern Variation (Critical for humanization)

**DO NOT write like this (uniform, AI-ish):**
"Our platform leverages advanced AI capabilities to deliver seamless automation solutions.
We help enterprises transform their workflows through intelligent process optimization.
Our cutting-edge technology enables businesses to achieve unprecedented efficiency gains."

**WRITE like this (varied, human, credible):**
"Building AI agents that actually work in production is harder than most vendors admit.
The gap between a demo and a production system handling real enterprise data, real edge cases,
and real user expectations — that gap is where most AI projects fail.
We've shipped over 40 agentic systems. Here's what we learned."

## Opening Paragraph Formulas (Avoid repeating the same formula mechanically)

**Formula 1 — The Specific Claim:**
"{Specific technical/market claim}. Here's why that matters for {persona}."

**Formula 2 — The Contrast:**
"{What everyone does}. {What actually works}."

**Formula 3 — The Insider Observation:**
"If you've tried to {do X} and hit {specific problem}, you've encountered {root cause}.
Most {solutions} don't address this. {Your approach} does."

**Formula 4 — The Stakes Frame:**
"{Trend} is not a future prediction — it's happening in production right now.
{Specific evidence}. Companies that {action} today will {outcome} by {timeframe}."

**Formula 5 — The Direct Assertion:**
"{Company/Product} builds {specific thing} for {specific customer}.
Not {what you're NOT}. {What you ARE}."

## H1 and H2 Writing Rules

### H1 Rules:
- Include the primary keyword or a natural close variant when it fits the reader's task.
- Keep it concise; 70 characters is an editorial starting point, not a ranking limit.
- Convey the page topic and, where useful, a benefit or differentiation — not just a keyword string.
- Avoid: "Welcome to X", "X Services", "About X"
- Good: "AI Agent Development for Enterprise Teams — Ship Faster, Fail Less"
- Good: "Build Autonomous AI Agents That Work in Production"
- Good: "Agentic AI Platform — From Prototype to Production in Weeks"

### H2 Rules:
- Use descriptive headings that match the section content; include keyword phrasing only where natural.
- H2s collectively should tell the story of the page (skim test: read only H2s, does it make sense?).
- Vary question and statement formats when useful; do not manufacture questions solely to target People Also Ask.
- Examples: "What Is {Topic}?", "How Does {X} Work?", "{Feature}: {Benefit}", "{Topic} — {Specific Angle}".

### H3 Rules:
- Support the parent H2 and add meaningful structure.
- They can be shorter and more specific; use them where they make the page easier to navigate.

## The Featured Snippet Formula

For a "What Is X?" section targeting a definition-style SERP, consider a concise, self-contained definition:

"{Primary keyword} is {category} that {differentiator}. Unlike {traditional alternative},
{primary keyword} {specific technical capability}. {Specific use case or result}."

A 40–60 word paragraph can be a useful starting point, not a requirement. Match the observed SERP, reader need and topic complexity; don't claim that Google always prefers a particular fixed length or format.

**Example:**
"Agentic AI is a category of artificial intelligence systems that autonomously plan,
decide, and execute multi-step tasks without constant human input. Unlike traditional AI
models that respond to single prompts, agentic AI maintains context across actions, uses
external tools, and self-corrects based on outcomes — making it viable for complex business workflows."

## FAQ Writing Rules

When the keyword/SERP research and page purpose justify an FAQ section:
- Select relevant questions from observed People Also Ask, support/sales questions, or real user research; don't invent PAA observations.
- Use as many questions as needed to cover distinct, useful questions. 6–10 can be a template for broad pillar pages, not a universal minimum.
- Answer directly and concisely; length depends on question complexity rather than a fixed "featured snippet sweet spot".
- Use natural question phrasing and avoid duplicate questions already answered fully in the main copy.
- Add internal links only where they genuinely help users continue their journey.

Do not add an FAQ section solely to satisfy a template or imply it guarantees a rich result.

**FAQ question format:**
"What is the difference between agentic AI and traditional AI automation?"
"How long does it take to build an AI agent for enterprise use?"
"What programming languages are used in AI agent development?"
"Can small businesses use AI agents, or is this enterprise-only?"
"What is Model Context Protocol (MCP) and why does it matter?"

## CTA Writing Rules

**Never write:**
- "Contact Us"
- "Learn More"
- "Get Started"
- "Click Here"
- "Request a Demo" (alone — too generic)

**Always write specific CTAs:**
- "Architect Your AI System →" (primary)
- "Talk to an AI Engineer →" (secondary)
- "Book a 30-Minute FDE Technical Scope Session →" (FDE pages)
- "Speak directly with a senior AI engineer to map your software requirements into an AI-native architecture." (subtext)
- "See How We Built a Multi-Agent System for a Series B SaaS"
- "Get Your AI Readiness Assessment — Free for 14 Days"
- "Download the AI-Native Company Playbook"

Primary CTA: High-commitment action (book call, start trial, get assessment)
Secondary CTA: Lower-commitment action (download resource, read case study, see examples)

---

## Internal Linking Rules

- Add contextual links to the next most useful page; there is no universal links-per-word ratio.
- Use descriptive, natural anchor text rather than empty labels such as "click here".
- Link to relevant supporting cluster pages, service/product pages, documentation and real case studies.
- Avoid forced links, repeated exact-match anchors and linking to pages that do not support the reader's task.
- Use the site's real URL map and verify every target; do not invent routes.
- Do not over-link to the homepage when an interior page is the better destination.

## E-E-A-T Signal Injection

### Experience Signals (use only when true, attributable and approved for publication):
- "We've built {verified number} AI agents for {type of companies}"
- "Across {verified project set}, the most common failure point was..."
- "When we shipped {verified project}, here's what we learned..."

These are writing patterns, not permission to imply experience. When the author has not personally done the work, frame it as research or a clearly labeled hypothetical scenario.

### Expertise Signals (sprinkle throughout):
- Reference real tools: LangChain, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, LlamaIndex, Haystack, Dify
- Reference real infrastructure: AWS Bedrock, Azure OpenAI, GCP Vertex AI
- Reference real databases: Pinecone, Weaviate, Qdrant, pgvector, ChromaDB
- Reference real evaluation: RAGAS, TruLens, Langfuse, Helicone
- Mention specific model names/versions only when current, relevant to the page, and checked against official documentation; do not name-drop models merely to sound technical.

### Authoritativeness Signals:
- Link to: arxiv papers, GitHub repos, official framework docs
- Reference: Stanford AI Index, McKinsey AI Report, Gartner AI research (with year)
- Mention specific conferences: NeurIPS, ICLR, MLOps World

### Trust Signals:
- Acknowledge real limitations honestly: "Not every use case needs agentic AI..."
- Use precise outcome figures only when verified and approved for publication; false precision is worse than a qualitative explanation.
- Include social proof format: {Company type} + {specific outcome} + {timeframe}

## Schema Markup Requirements

Recommend structured data that matches the actual page and the visible content. Do not force the same schema types onto every page, and do not promise rankings or rich results because markup is present. Before making a Google rich-result recommendation, check Google's current Search Gallery and structured-data policies.

**Organization schema:** use where it accurately describes the organization and its official identity; validate which page is the right home for the canonical organization entity. Do not blindly duplicate conflicting Organization entities on every URL.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "name": "{Company Name}",
  "description": "{Description}",
  "url": "https://{domain}",
  "sameAs": ["{LinkedIn}", "{GitHub}", "{Twitter}"]
}
```

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Service",
  "name": "{Service Name}",
  "provider": {"@type": "Organization", "name": "{Company}"},
  "description": "{Service description}",
  "serviceType": "{AI Agent Development / etc}"
}
```

**Pages with a real FAQ section:** FAQPage JSON-LD may be used when it faithfully marks up visible question-and-answer content and complies with current policies. Do not require it for all pages or promise Google FAQ rich-result exposure.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "{Question}",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "{Answer}"
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

## The AI Search Optimization Layer

For every piece of content, apply these AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) rules:

1. **Entity clarity**: Identify the company/product/service early where brand or service clarity is useful for the page's intent.
2. **Claim-answer pairing**: Place supporting evidence near material factual claims; don't imply every marketing sentence needs a citation.
3. **Definition anchors**: Explain technical terms when the likely reader may not know them.
4. **Direct answer formatting**: Give a direct answer before details where that structure serves the query.
5. **Cite-worthy evidence**: Include statistics or measured data only when relevant and verified; never add numbers just to meet a quota.
6. **Source linking**: Link to primary/high-quality sources when they substantiate material factual claims. A fixed source count is not a substitute for relevance or credibility.

## Meta Tag Templates

### Title Tag (suggested 50–60 character starting point; not a hard ranking rule):
"{Primary KW} — {Differentiator} | {Brand}"
"{Primary KW} for {Persona}: {Benefit} | {Brand}"
"Build {X} with {Technology} — {Brand}"

### Meta Description (suggested 140–160 character starting point; display length varies):
"{Primary KW} built for {persona}. {Key benefit}. {Specific capability}. {CTA}."
"We build {X} that {outcome}. {Differentiator from alternatives}. {CTA}."

### OG Tags:
og:title — Same as title tag or slight variant (can be slightly longer)
og:description — Same as meta description (or expand to 200 chars)
og:image — 1200×630px, text overlay with primary keyword visible

## Tone Matrix

Use this as a loose house-style reference, not a literal percentage quota:

- Confident and direct: 'We build X, not Y'
- Technically specific: 'Our orchestration layer handles X via Y'
- Forward-looking only when relevant: discuss real market/technical changes with current evidence
- Humble and honest: acknowledge limitations and cases where a different approach is better

Avoid these words: 'premier', 'leading', '#1', 'best-in-class', 'world-class', 'top-tier', 'innovative' (unless truly novel), 'next-generation' (overused)

---

## Proof-Before-Pitch Architecture

- When relevant, introduce real, approved proof before a broad capability pitch.
- A service/enterprise landing page may benefit from 2–3 concrete workflow examples, but do not fabricate deployment details, customer outcomes, durations, volumes or metrics.
- If verified customer evidence is unavailable, use an explicitly labeled illustrative workflow or a transparent explanation of the method; do not imply it happened in production.
- Template:
  ```
  ## Built for Real [Industry/Domain] Workflows

  Use one of the following only when supported by the evidence inventory:
  * **[Verified workflow]:** [specific action] + [client-approved measure/source] + [documented outcome]
  * **[Illustrative workflow]:** [hypothetical scenario clearly identified as illustrative]
  ```

---

## Anti-Self-Proclamation Rules

- BANNED words/phrases: 'premier', 'leading', '#1', 'best-in-class', 'world-class', 'top-tier', 'industry-leading', 'unmatched', 'unparalleled'
- Replace with: specific technical architecture descriptions
- Example bad: 'We are the leading AI development company'
- Example good: 'We engineer autonomous AI agents using LangGraph supervisor topologies with MCP tool servers'

---

## No Fabricated Evidence Rules

- NEVER cite statistics without verified sources
- NEVER use 'According to Stanford/Gartner/McKinsey' without the actual report name and year
- Use operational benchmarks only when measured or approved by the client. Otherwise describe the workflow without inventing a duration or percentage.
- If client provides verified metrics, mark them as [CLIENT-VERIFIED] in the draft

---

## Hero Value-First Template

- Make the offer, intended buyer and outcome clear early; answer “What do I get?” as well as “What does this company do?”
- Use a compact “What You Get” deliverables block when it improves clarity; the ASCII box elsewhere in the skill is a planning sketch, not required website formatting.
- Make the primary CTA specific to the actual offer and commitment level. “Architect Your AI System” and “Talk to an AI Engineer” are examples, not mandatory phrases. “Contact us” can be suitable if that is the clearest accurate action for the business.

---

## Comparison Table Safety

- Never label client's own legacy brand negatively
- Use generic competitor labels: 'Traditional Agency Model', 'Generic AI Agency'
- Never use the word 'obsolete' for client's own past services

---

## Center-of-Gravity Rule

- Give each page one dominant positioning so its purpose is unmistakable. The 70/30 split is a useful planning heuristic from this workflow, not a measurable keyword/ranking requirement.
- Supporting services should not dilute the primary promise; the actual amount depends on the page and user's decision process.
- Give primary topics suitable depth; summarize secondary services or link out when that best serves the reader.
